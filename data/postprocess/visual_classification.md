# Visual Classification: Politik in der Demokratie Questions

**Total questions analyzed:** 242  
**Questions with visual groupings:** ~180  
**Text-only questions:** ~60

---

## Groups that benefit from a shared visual

### 🏛️ Group 1 — Structure of Government / Three Branches (Gewaltenteilung)
**Recommended visual:** Separation of powers triangle (Legislative → Judikative → Exekutive)

**Questions:** 32, 33, 51, 52, 53, 54, 60, 63, 65, 88, 143, 144, 145

These all ask what belongs to which branch, what Rechtsstaat means, Volkssouveränität, and which body is/isn't part of the state power. A single Gewaltenteilung diagram would anchor all of them.

---

### 🗳️ Group 2 — The Bundestag, Bundesrat, and Federal Organs
**Recommended visual:** Federal organs chart (Bundestag, Bundesrat, Bundeskanzler, Bundespräsident, Bundesverfassungsgericht)

**Questions:** 42, 44, 48, 57, 58, 60, 65, 74, 80, 81, 82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 87, 89, 90, 98, 129

These ask who elects whom, who appoints ministers, what the Bundesrat is, what Fraktionen are, etc. A single org-chart style diagram of how the federal institutions relate would cover all of them.

---

### 🗳️ Group 3 — Elections and the Voting System
**Recommended visual:** How a Bundestagswahl ballot works (Erststimme / Zweitstimmen, 5%-Hürde, proportional seats)

**Questions:** 5, 28, 93, 94, 107, 108, 109, 110, 112, 113, 114, 115, 116, 117, 119, 120, 121, 122, 123, 124, 125, 126, 127, 128, 129, 130

These ask about free/secret/equal elections, the 5%-Hürde, Zweitstimmen, how many years, who may vote, what a valid ballot looks like. A labelled sample ballot + seat allocation diagram covers the whole cluster.

---

### 🏘️ Group 4 — Federal Structure / Administrative Levels
**Recommended visual:** 3-level administrative pyramid (Bund → Länder → Gemeinden)

**Questions:** 24, 49, 56, 64, 67, 68, 69, 131

These ask how many Bundesländer there are, who controls school policy, what the lowest administrative level is, and what a Bürgermeister does. A simple pyramid with examples at each level would help.

---

### ⚖️ Group 5 — Fundamental Rights (Grundrechte / Grundgesetz)
**Recommended visual:** Grundgesetz first page + rights list, or a spider diagram of rights

**Questions:** 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20

These all ask about rights guaranteed in the Basic Law, what Rechtsstaat means at the rights level, what is/isn't in the Grundgesetz, and limits on free speech. A single rights overview visual would contextualise all of them.

---

### 🇩🇪 Group 6 — Symbols and Identity (Flagge, Wappen, Hymne)
**Recommended visual:** Flag + eagle crest + first line of anthem

**Questions:** 21, 29, 40, 214, 216

Very direct symbol-recognition questions. One composite image answers all five.

---

### 🏛️ Group 7 — Parties, Coalitions, and Party Acronyms
**Recommended visual:** German party logos/colour spectrum (left–right) + coalition diagram

**Questions:** 13, 30, 31, 41, 43, 73, 76, 78, 79, 88, 92, 98, 103

These ask what Opposition means, why there are multiple parties, when a party can be banned, what CDU/SPD/FDP/CSU stand for, and what Ampelkoalition means. A party spectrum + coalition example diagram works here.

---

### 🧑‍⚖️ Group 8 — Courts and the Judiciary
**Recommended visual:** Court hierarchy / Rechtsweg

**Questions:** 80, 136, 137, 139, 140, 141, 142, 143, 144, 145, 146, 147, 148, 150

These ask about Richter, Schöffen, Rechtsanwälte, what court handles labour disputes, what a Prozess is, and which court interprets the Grundgesetz. A Rechtsweg (court hierarchy) diagram clarifies the structure behind all of them.

---

### 🇪🇺 Group 9 — Germany in Europe / EU
**Recommended visual:** Map of Germany + neighbours + EU flag, EU Parliament seating

**Questions:** 173, 221, 222, 223, 224, 225, 226, 227, 229, 230, 231, 232, 233, 234, 235, 236, 237, 238, 239, 240

These ask about EU membership, Schengen, the Euro, where the EU Parliament sits, which countries are neighbours, and what the EU flag looks like. A map + EU symbol composite covers the whole group.

## Key Insights

- **High-impact visuals** address structural and systems-based questions (government, elections, courts, rights)
- **One diagram can anchor ~20-30 questions** when they share a common conceptual framework
- **Text-only questions** tend to be abstract definitions, current facts, or sensitive topics
- **For visual learners**, starting with the 9 core diagrams would provide strong foundational understanding before diving into specific question sets
